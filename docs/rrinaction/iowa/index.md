# Obermann Center for Advanced Studies Summer Internship Program 

In Spring 2026, the Obermann Center for Advanced Studies invited University of Iowa graduate students in humanities and humanities-adjacent programs to apply for paid summer internships with Iowa City nonprofit organizations [Public Space One](https://www.publicspaceone.com/), [Neighborhood Centers of Johnson County](https://ncjc.org/index.html), and the [Iowa City Public Library Friends Foundation](https://supporticpl.org/). Six positions were available (two at each site). This eight-week summer program gave UI graduate students the chance to gain hands-on professional experience outside the classroom while building skills that translate across many career paths. Interns applied humanities training such as research, writing, critical thinking, and ethical analysis in real world, public-facing settings. The program is for humanities and humanities-adjacent fields where career pathways beyond the tenure track are typically less defined within graduate training. 
The program also offered structured support for career exploration and professional growth. Through cohort meetings and workshops, students developed practical tools for the job market while connecting with peers and mentors. 

## Program Benefits
* Eight-week summer internship in June and July 2026 with $5,000 taxable stipend
* Professional experience with a local non-profit
* Cohort meetings and workshops focused on resumes, CVs, cover letters, informational interviews, career exploration, assessment, and grant searching and writing
* Mentorship from non-profit professionals and Obermann Center staff
* A supportive cohort of fellow humanities graduate students

Interns participated in a late spring orientation and met every two weeks as a cohort throughout the summer to reflect on their experiences, share insights, and build confidence navigating diverse career pathways.

## Staffing
The Obermann Associate Director led the creation and implementation of the program. She worked with the community partners to develop internship descriptions, facilitated the matching process in collaboration with the Obermann Director and community partners, and created and oversaw orientation and cohort meetings. Obermann Director of Operations oversaw grant budget and processing of payments and use of grant funds. Community partners oversaw student work.

> ### Public Space One Media Arts Co-op Archive and Storytelling Intern
> *Position Description*: This position supports research, storytelling, and public engagement related to the Media Arts Co-op’s (MAC) public access television archives. This internship focuses on activating and interpreting archival materials from Iowa City’s former public access television channel (PATV) and sharing stories drawn from this collection with a broad public audience. PS1’s Media Arts Co-op program grew out of the closure of Iowa City’s public access television station and its subsequent merger with PS1. As part of PS1’s ongoing commitment to the legacy of PATV, this internship centers on telling the story of public access media in Iowa City and its broader cultural impact. The PATV archives include over 700 VHS tapes, thousands of hours of digital video, photographs, and paper materials documenting decades of community-produced media. These materials capture local arts and culture, community journalism, experimental video, public conversations, and everyday life in Iowa City, while also connecting to a national movement for public access media. The intern will build on prior archival work and community programs to develop several publicly accessible stories drawn from the archives and new research.
>
> *Responsibilities*:
> * Review and assess materials within the PATV archives
> * Build on previous archival projects and recent PS1 programming
> * Research comparable public access and community media archive projects nationally
> * Conduct short interviews with local PATV participants and Media Arts Co-op members
> * Develop 3-5 multimedia narratives (text, images, video, or hybrid formats)
> * Publish completed stories on the Public Media One website
>
> *Deliverables*:
> * Consider the current state of the archive and envision its future
> * Identify a portion of the archive to more fully engage, to build on previous archival projects and recent PS1 programming
> * Synthesize a portion of the archival materials and more recent dialogues into accessible narratives
> * Create at least 3 public-facing narratives shared with the PS1, PATV, and local community
> * Draft a set of written recommendations describing the PATV Archives program, scope, and potential opportunities for future expansion and engagement
