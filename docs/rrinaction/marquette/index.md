# Capacity Milwaukee

Through the methodological principles outlined in HWW’s Reciprocity and Redistribution in Action grant project, Marquette University created the Capacity Milwaukee Summer Fellowship to enhance nonprofit organizational research capabilities and to build methods of community collaboration in Milwaukee. Our design prioritized the methodological principles outlined in research practice partnerships (RPP), a scholarly model developed between scholars of education with K-12 educational practitioners. Our program facilitated a humanities based RPP approach connecting humanities scholars with nonprofit practitioners in Milwaukee working on a variety of social justice endeavors across the city.

The Capacity Milwaukee Fellows included: Fereshteh Majdi and Kate Rose, doctoral students in the English department; and Melady Elifritz and Karolyn Burns, doctoral students in the Philosophy department. Fereshteh Majdi was matched with the Milwaukee Turners, the oldest civic organization in Milwaukee whose history dates back to 1853, and they built a rubric which helps the Turners to assess the impact of Turners’ public wellness programming through social determinants of health metrics. Kate Rose partnered with Ex Fabula, a humanities-based nonprofit which creates public programming through storytelling methods, and they developed an oral history archive from interviews Kate conducted with those impacted by Ex Fabula’s programming. Melady Elifritz worked with ARCHS (Action Research Community High School) to create narrative marketing materials supporting enrollment efforts for the new community high school. ARCHS is working to create a project-based education model for Milwaukee students living on the city’s south side. Finally, Karolyn Burns worked alongside Metcalfe Park Community Bridges, a neighborhood community organization dedicated to creating programming which uplifts residents from one of the most impacted communities harmed by mass incarceration and an emerging food desert crisis. Karolyn collaboratively wrote a policy white paper with Metcalfe that detailed neighborhood designed recommendations to the city of Milwaukee, officially offering the city the community’s solution for addressing the crisis related to grocery store closures in 2025.

In February 2025, Marquette University proposed a new AmeriCorps program grant to support Milwaukee’s nonprofit community and to match them with a doctoral student enrolled in humanities PhD programs at the university. As career diversity programming grows across the nation, data demonstrates that employers increasingly seek candidates with direct internship experience prior to employing them in their organizations, and our program has a second goal to build a meaningful internship experience for the fellows. Marquette is committed to developing internships which aid graduate students and their career development. They not only gain meaningful work experiences but learn more about Milwaukee and the needs of nonprofits in the community prior to graduation.

We initially developed the Capacity Milwaukee Fellowship with the support of an AmeriCorps planning grant, and the program model would have funded community-based internship experiences. Based on lessons from HWW, the Graduate School sought the community’s voice in the fellowship’s design and hosted a community listening session in 2025 with Milwaukee’s nonprofit organizations to build rapport, trust, and to define nonprofit needs. Our goal at that time was to collaboratively co-design the program’s service intervention for future AmeriCorps members, and over sixty organizations attended this listening session. Nonprofits also took part in a survey collecting their thoughts on the program’s design, to share their organizational needs, and to assess their thoughts about the value of the proposed program. Sadly, the AmeriCorps planning grant ended abruptly with federal funding cuts in 2025.

HWW’s R&R in Action grant provided an opportunity to reimagine the AmeriCorps project by providing seed funds in summer 2026 to test the program model’s design, and the summer fellowship experiences reinforced our initial hopes about the program’s potential impact when we first designed it in 2025. In this pilot model with funding from HWW, we edited the program for a three-month experience and one which could also redistribute money to community partners. When asked to describe the summer experience and role in their career development, fellows reported that it was “empowering” and said, “it was helpful to see what my PhD could do for me” by working outside the academy, meeting with people they otherwise would not have opportunities to meet, and to learn “now I know I can do hard things” and work beyond the academy. For one fellow, the summer fellowship empowered them to “know there is work for me after this experience.” Another fellow also shared that “it’s been my goal since I entered into Marquette as a PhD to always center community and this opportunity gave me that chance to put my money where my mouth is.” For all the community partners, the collaboration allowed them to create projects they would otherwise not have been able to build without the work of the fellows and created community-led research projects as well as community owned knowledge production. This project is distinct from other community-based projects at the university because it resides at a nexus between the intersection of graduate student professional development and capacity building for community partners. Both the fellows and the partners had an active role in the production of research and scholarship through the fellowship experience.

Three full-time Marquette staff members worked on the project in a secondary capacity from November 2025 to September 2026. While the fellowship experience was three months long from June to September 2026, the coordination, application, and review process for selecting (1) fellows; (2) non-profit partners; and (3) making placement selections, took about four months to complete. Staff members included project lead, Maggie Nettesheim Hoffmann, Director of the Center for the Advancement of the Humanities; Alisha Klapps Balistreri, Assistant Director of the Trinity Fellowship program; and Freddie Gonzales, Records Coordinator in Marquette’s Graduate School.

## Fellow's Biographies

**Kate Rose** is a PhD candidate in the English department at Marquette University. Her dissertation project, *Affirmation/Transformation: Fandom Created*, began with an exhibition of fanworks inspired by fine art, which ran at the Haggerty Museum of Art from August 23-December 21, 2024, and will eventually expand to include an online archive and academic repository of and about those works. The project explores fandom as a space of creativity, labor, and community. Both the original exhibition and the resulting online archive function simultaneously as a public humanities initiative introducing fandom and fan studies to audiences outside of academia, an archive of fanworks across media and genre, and an exploration of what fans create and what they receive in return for their labor. Kate presented on her project at the 2026 at the Annual Pop Culture Association conference in Atlanta, and has a forthcoming publication for *The New Americanist*, both of which focus on the creation and impact of *Affirmation/Transformation*. She is currently anticipating graduating in December 2027. Kate was matched with [Ex Fabula](https://www.exfabula.org/).

**Melady Elifritz** *(she/they)* is a multi-various educator/researcher, birth worker, and working-class single mother of two small children. She is currently a doctoral candidate in philosophy at Marquette University and an adjunct faculty member at Midwives College of Utah. She works largely in social/political philosophy with an emphasis on Marxist and Black feminisms, and decolonial and queer theory. Her dissertation explores the political status of pregnant people and the birthing process as they are both subject to and resist racial capitalism’s obstetric violences. This summer she has a Capacity MKE Fellowship. When not talking excitedly about pregnancy and birth justice, she is usually working in her community garden plot or organizing all the toddler parents in her neighborhood. Melady worked with [ARCHS (Action Research Community High School)](https://www.archsmke.org/).

**Fereshteh Majdi** has just completed her first year as a Ph.D. student in English at Marquette University. Her research examines the relationship between literature and social movements, with a particular interest in how ideas circulate between cultural texts and collective action. She is especially interested in speculative fiction, and the ways literature can imagine alternative social and political futures. Originally from Iran, she moved to the United States in 2022 to pursue graduate studies and previously earned an M.A. in English from Kansas State University, where she taught first-year writing courses. As a Capacity Milwaukee Fellow, Fereshteh is excited to collaborate on a community-engaged project that bridges academic research and public impact. She is particularly interested in exploring how research and critical thinking skills developed in academia can be applied beyond the university to address real-world challenges and contribute to meaningful social change. Fereshteh worked with the [Milwaukee Turners](https://www.milwaukeeturners.org/).

**Karolyn Burns** *(they/them)* is a PhD candidate in Philosophy at Marquette University. Their research interests include the study of curiosity, creativity, and community making online and offline. Karolyn has experience serving as a facilitator, reciprocal researcher, and connector between local nonprofits and universities. As a direct result of their community centered work, Karolyn is the recipient of several fellowships including the prestigious HWW-Capacity MKE Summer Fellowship, HWW-Mellon Predoctoral Fellowship and the Teaching Excellence award from Marquette University. They are currently an adjunct at Milwaukee Area Technical College and the University of Wisconsin, Milwaukee where they craft and engage in courses centered on curiosity and the ethics of technology. Karolyn collaborated with [Metcalfe Park Community Bridges](https://metcalfeparkbridges.org/).
