# Grounded Knowledge

*"If the future of knowledge is not wisdom but 'upgrade,' where might we look for humanity's own future? Isn't it reasonable to assume that projecting earthly human life into the future may not be the disaster movie we are constantly invited to enjoy, but a configuration of what we are here for?"* 
Toni Morrison, "The Habit of Art" (2010)

**Grounded Knowledge** is a graduate student summer internship program rooted in communities of struggle, where the ethos and framework for reciprocity and redistribution are put into practice to animate dynamic knowledge production. This program was created by the Liberal Arts Engagement Hub in the College of Liberal Arts (The Hub) at the University of Minnesota with the grant from Humanities Without Walls (HWW), a consortium of humanities centers located at the University of Illinois, Urbana-Champaign. 

HWW’s commitment to “Reciprocity and Redistribution in Action” (R&R in Action) is a key pacesetter. Grounded Knowledge advances a dynamic public humanities initiative through the following interrelated process: (1) enfolding non-extractive and non-exploitative ways of engaging with community partners, many of whom are creative artists, into higher education by harnessing principles of reciprocity and redistribution and (2) guiding PhD students into this domain of R&R in Action to catalyze exciting formations and set new norms for institutional practices of community engagement simultaneously.

This orientation aligns with The Hub’s mission, vision, and commitments. Grounded Knowledge has allowed The Hub to make an inroad into College of Liberal Arts (CLA) graduate education and at the same time reinforce our strengths. We are committed to opening the space, wherein CLA scholars, staff, and students and community partners enmeshed in social justice struggles can come together to respond to the still unmet political challenge of creating a more egalitarian, humane, and just society. Since 2021, The Hub has cultivated such dynamic relationships with local organizations. These relationships have helped position The Hub to co-create public humanities internships that will benefit the organizations and their communities, while offering opportunities for graduate students in the humanities to engage with historical injustices that pulsate in the present.

Guided by the direction and leadership of Amanda Steepleton, Assistant Director of The Hub, we built a solid administrative structure. We recruited four PhD students: Nicole Bekesz (Geography); Tasneem Alzamara (Feminist Studies), Fatemeh NasrEsfahani (Feminist Studies), and Mohammadreza (Reza) Izadi Hemmatabadi (Theatre Historiography and Performance Studies). Each student interned at an organization with ties to The Hub, namely Amplify MN, The Southeast Asian Diaspora (SEAD) Project, and Soomaal House of Art. They worked for 10 weeks from June 8 to August 14 and participated in several “grounding sessions” to strengthen our collective commitment to R&R in Action.

Throughout, we were guided by the following question: “How can we who are wedded to institutional mores and expectations (i.e., academic professionalization) follow in the footsteps of artists and creative people who reside adjacent to and outside the university to make knowledge for the future world?” This question gets to the core of intellectual challenge that Toni Morrison refers to as “a reconfiguration of what we are here for” (see the quoted passage above). At its core, *Grounded Knowledge* is conceived as a forum to hone our orientation to this strategic practice. Our position is as follows:
* First: CLA graduate students in the arts and humanities are eager to rethink and rework knowledge production that is designed solely to validate higher education and its institutional mores, including the very idea of professionalization. Many are trained to wrestle with power, especially power imbalances between the university and community.
* Second: Their readiness to follow in the footsteps of artists and people in struggle who reside adjacent to and outside the university is palpable.
* Third: Being *adjacent to* and *outside* are two distinct orientations to knowledge; both are necessary to locate the grounded knowledge of creative people. CLA graduate students are not only attuned to this fact but also interested in learning how to harness it within higher education without being extractive and possessive.
* Fourth: The Hub is uniquely positioned to generate this new knowledge because we have been steadfast in shaping the space where humanistic scholars in CLA and the community come together to practice transformative community engagement within higher education.
* If *Grounded Knowledge* has a thesis statement, it will be the following: *the arts and humanities can become the antidote to our perilous times.*
