The processes for matching and placing students will vary across institutions and be shaped by a variety of factors, including but not limited to:
* Exisitng infrastructure for service learning;
* Relationships with community organizations and their desired level of involvement in the application/selection process;
* Needs identified by the organizations an intern can fulfill

Some of the R&R in Action programs at HWW consortial partner institutions identified organziations before recruiting applications from students and shaped the selection criteria around the needs the organization specified. In some cases, the organziations interviewed applicants as part of the placement process. 

The HWW Summer Bridge Program selected students first, then their information is immediately shared with our partners at WeCU, who then sent the students a project matching survey where students were asked to rank causes and issues they are most interested in. WeCU used the survey information to identify local organizations and began contacting them to put the internship in place. The decision to structure placement in this way is based on the infrastructure established by WeCU in working with service learning students and their longstanding relationships with local community organizations. 

When the interns begin working with their partner organizations, the organization will orient them during their first week. Prior to that, in the late spring semester, students should also attend a program orientation with the coordinator to cover the methods and ethos of the program, logistics and schedule, meet their cohort members, and address any questions or issues that need to be resolved before the start of the program.
