**Placing Students with Community Partners**

*If Community Partners are Identified Before Students are Selected*

If your program has already determined which organizations students will work with, the process of selecting and placing students with community partner organizations should be developed in conversation between the program coordinator(s) and community partners. You may choose to have a committee review applications and identify students for the partner organizations to interview, or have partners on the review committee and select students without holding interviews. These strategies allow the needs of the organizations to determine which students are selected and the types of skillsets that make strong applicants stand out. 

*If Students are Selected Before Community Partners are Identified*

The Summer Bridge Program selects students before identifying community partners. This method, chosen in consultation with the University of Illinois's service learning units (WeCU and the Community Learning Lab (CLL)), allows us to identify students with skillsets that would appeal to a variety of organizations and use a project matching process to pair them with a partner. After students are selected, we send them a project matching survey to begin this process. The survey gives them a list of causes/populations and asks them to rank the top 5 they are most interested in working with. This list is based on the organizations in Champaign-Urbana and their focus areas. The survey also asks them for a short bio to share with organizations, as well as the skills existing skills they could contribute (such as grant writing, curriculum development, assessment, etc.), skills they are interested in developing, and if they speak any languages other than English (and their level of fluency). The CLL then uses the survey responses to determine what organizations might be a good fit and sends a list of 4-5 for the student to rank. From there, CLL begins contacting the organizations in the student's ranked order and shares their bio, skillsets, and asks if they would benefit from/have the capacity to host an intern for the summer. Below is sample timeline for this process:
* April 3: Deadline for students to complete project matching survey
* April 1-13: Identification of potential partners, sending list of options for students to rank (students respond within 3 business days)
* April 13-May 15: Reaching out to community partners
* May 1-15: Community partner introductions go out on rolling basis as matches are confirmed; students and partner will set up an introductory meeting
* May 18: Students begin working with community partner
