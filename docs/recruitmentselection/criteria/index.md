## *If Community Partners are Identified Before Students are Selected*

If your program has already determined which community organizations will host summer interns before reviewing student applications, the selection of students should be informed by needs of the organization as well as by how well the students understand the centrality of reciprocal and redistributive methods. As you develop the application, ask organizations what they imagine the students will be working on and the kinds of skills that would be most beneficial in an intern. Consider also the skillsets of the organization's staff and what kinds of skills they might be able to mentor the intern in. For example, if an organization has 30 years worth of program materials, photos, and other documents, a student with experience in archiving practices might be a good fit. In addition to organizing and archiving, the student might collaborate with the organization's grant writer or finance manager to learn more about securing funding for archival projects. 

## *If Students are Selected Before Community Partners are Identified*

If your program is selecting students first then pairing them with a community partner, your review criteria may be more general in terms of the skillsets sought but more heavily focused on a deep understanding of community engagement as reciprocal and redistributive. This understanding almost always coincides with flexibility and adaptability, which are valuable traits in community engaged work. As your committee reviews applications, you will want to evaluate the student's mindset on community engagement (do they see this as an opportunity to "give back," or are they hoping to learn from local experts while sharing their skills?), and also take note of particular skillsets that might appeal to organizations (oral history experience, digital or physical archiving skills, technological skills such as web development/design or coding, grant writing experience, etc.).
