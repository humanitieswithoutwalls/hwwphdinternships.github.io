**Eligibility**

Program eligibility may vary depending on the aim and scope of different graduate internship opportunities. Considerations for student eligibility should include type and discipline of graduate program, stage of progress through degree requirements, enrollment status vis-à-vis internship appointment, projected graduation date, work eligibility/visa status, residency, and others. In the example of the HWW Summer Bridge Program, eligibilty is limited to humanities PhD students (both pre- and ABD) who are enrolled the semester prior to summer work, who plan to graduate no sooner than the semester following the summer term, and who reside near enough to campus to conduct in-person work at a local organization. 

**Timeline**

To recruit applicants for the HWW Summer Bridge Program, publicity begins late in the fall semester. The application is posted to the website in October/November and our communications staff include it in weekly newsletters from then until the applications are due, along with a link to register for an info, which is held in January. Applications are then due in late February with selection and notification taking place in early March. Please see the example timeline page for more detail on planning out a program.

**Info Session**

An info session is a great way for potential applicants to learn more about the program and evaluate whether it is a good fit for them. This session should give a detailed overview of the program and its goals, methods, and ethos, and should detail what good candidates look like. The Summer Bridge Program info session walks students through the application and gives tips on how to narrate their experiences and skillsets in ways that are appealing to community organizations. We also invite a former Bridge participant to share a bit about their experience and some of the things they learned during their internship. You can find a past Bridge info session on the [program page](https://www.humanitieswithoutwalls.illinois.edu/summer-bridge-program/summer-bridge-program-call-applications). 

**Application**

When designing the application for a program, it is important to consider the kinds of information community partners want to know about students in addition to asking questions about the student's personal philosophy and approach to community-engaged work. If you have communnity organization partners identified prior to opening the application to students, your strategy may be to solicit application questions from them, or co-develop the application. If students have the option to apply to work with specific partners, having multiple applications with input from the organization may help ensure the best student placement. If you have not pre-determined partners before accepting applications, keeping the questions more general but focused on the student's mindset toward community-engagement may be your best strategy. The Summer Bridge Program uses the following questions:
* What skills, areas of knowledge, or ways of looking at the world could you use to help a community organization make a positive impact? How could you foresee applying your humanistic training to advance an organization's mission?
* How would this experience contribute to your career development? What do you hope to learn from working with a community organization in the summer? What skills would you like to gain or develop?
* Describe a time where you collaborated with others to solve a problem.
Additionally, the application also requires a resume/CV and a recommendation form. This form is to be filled out by one person able to speak to the student's capacity and potential to contribute to a community organization (and does not have to be affiliated with the university).
